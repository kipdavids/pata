- `Find the number. Give your rival the clock.` (45)

## Description (448 / 500 characters)

```
PATA is a two-player number hunt. One hundred numbers, shuffled every match. One of you picks a target; the other races to find it on a 10x10 board. Every microsecond the finder takes gets added to the picker score. Then you swap. Five, ten or twenty turns each - most points wins.

No signup. Share a link, your friend taps it, you are playing. It installs to your home screen like an app, and it is free all the way: no ads, no coins, no paywall.
```

## Maker's first comment

This is the part people actually read, so it should sound like a person, not a
pitch deck.

```
I wanted a game I could play with one other person in the time it takes to boil
water. Not a lobby, not a queue, not a Discord server. Just: send a link, play
ten turns, talk about something else.

PATA is what came out of that. 100 numbers on a 10x10 grid, shuffled fresh each
match. On your turn you pick a number from the board. Your opponent then has to
find that same number, and the clock is running. Whatever time they take is
literally your score, measured in microseconds. Then the roles flip. Highest
total after the agreed turns wins.

A few things I cared about:

- Two players. Not two-to-eight, not "solo mode with a leaderboard." One person
  on each side, both doing something.
- No account. You get a guest name and a six-character code. That's the whole
  onboarding.
- Fair timing. The clock lives in the database, not in your browser, so nobody
  wins because their phone is faster.
- Free in the boring sense. No ads, no energy bars, no "watch this to play
  again." It's a small game and it stays that way.

It runs in the browser, installs to your home screen, and if your connection
blips it keeps the match alive.

Honest caveat: it is a simple game. There's no story, no progression tree, no
shop. It's one good idea about turn-taking and time, and a board. If that sounds
like your kind of thing, grab a friend and play a round. I'd love to hear what
feels off — especially the turn length and how the board reads on a phone.

Thanks for looking.
```

## A note on tone

Keep the launch like the game: short, plain, and confident about one idea. Avoid
"revolutionary," "addictive," "next-gen," and superlatives about the tech stack.
The strongest lines here are the plain ones — "no ads, no coins, no paywall" and
"share a link, your friend taps it, you're playing."
